Collocation Analysis of “倭” Represent throughout Ming Shi 明史
LAW Tsz-kin, Ken

-   This study uses collocation analysis to track changing patterns in a Ming corpus, revealing how the term “倭” is represented throughout Ming Shi 明史. Ming Shi is selected as the primary source for analysis; the complete digital text dataset consists of 9.26 MB of text with 24,597 words. The source is retrieved from public GitHub repository: qh-starter by Maciej Kurzynski, with standard copyright law applying (All Rights Reserved). Although the primary discussion of '倭' is in Chapter 322, a quick search reveals that the character appears throughout the entire text. Therefore, rather than isolating individual chapters, the entire corpus file was processed from end to end. In this article, Ming Shi is chosen for two reasons: First, Ming Shi is considered as an official historical text of the relevant dynasty, which means it is reliable. Second, the text contains historical content only from Ming Dynasty, so the data will not include irrelevant material from other periods of history. 
-   Before the analysis, Ming Shi was converted into simplified Chinese to ensure compatibility with Chinese word segmentation using jieba. With jieba, the text was then segmented into sentences with spaces removed for analysis. The target word “倭” was selected for collocation analysis, as it usually serves as an exonym for ancient Japan. 
-   According to Tognini-Bonelli (2001), there are two patterns that are distinguished: colligation captures the immediate syntactic relations, used to identify words within grammatical patterns, while collocation evaluates broader co-occurring words, which is useful for identifying collocates’ position and variability within given sentences. These arguments lead to two methods used in this study, in which a 5-word window focuses on colligation close to the target word, while a 10-word window focuses on collocation in the broader contextual structure. Besides these two methods, the sentence window was used to elaborate not only on specific events but also to identify their causes or consequences. Then, to exclude function words that lack substantive semantic content, a stopwords filter from qhchina was applied. This template consists of stopwords from classical Chinese texts (both traditional and simplified script), examples of stopwords include “之”, “者”, “也”, etc.
-   To evaluate these co-occurrences, a contingency table, p-value, log-likelihood and log-dice were used as indicators in the analysis. A contingency table calculates the ratio of the frequency of the co-occurrence of specific historical events or specific historical figures in the archives to the frequency of their individual occurrences. The resulting ratio, referred to as the p-value, tests the null hypothesis of independence, measuring whether the frequency of co-occurring words is statistically significant or the result of random chance; the results selected for discussion should have a p-value of less than 0.05. Another indicator is log-likelihood which is used to measure statistical significance; a high value shows that it is by no means a coincidence that the two words appear together. Moreover, log-dice measures the specificity, attraction and strength between two words; a high value on this measure indicates typicality between the two words.
-   After examining the collocates of the target word “倭”, results indicate that there is a different pattern between significance and strength measurement even with the same set of collocates. As mentioned above, log-likelihood and log-dice are used to measure significance and strength respectively. -   Looking across table 2, some collocates have nearly identical log-dice values across all windows, for example, 王京 and 海上. This implies that these collocates are highly stable when co-occurrence involves the target word and are not affected by expansion of windows. However, some other collocates have different behavioral patterns. Some collocates experience significant increases in both log-dice and frequency measures, for example, 朝鮮 and 釜山. This may indicate that the collocates exist in a broader contextual structure surrounding the target word. 
-   Overall, this table highlights how window size influences observed frequency and association strength. It indicates that 10-word analysis is more sensitive to patterns by capturing collocates that appear farther away from the target, while 5-word analysis is more sensitive to immediate associations. Shifts mainly occur between 5-word and 10-word analysis, while sentence analysis shows no notable significant differences when compared to 10-word analysis. 
-   Key word in context (KWIC) was used to analyze patterns of target collocates within the sentences or words surrounding them. Two words - 王京 and “新” - were used in this analysis, 王京 was chosen because it ranked top in most of the measures, while “新” was chosen because it is a frequent co-occurring single word. A 10-word window was chosen for KWIC analysis, as it covers broader patterns, and provides a clearer picture of events surrounding the target word.
-   The KWIC lines surrounding 王京 indicate that this is a place of high military value. The evidence that supports the argument is that the phrase 王京 usually has a verb before it, for example, “逼王京”, “守王京”, etc, and these verbs usually associate with military actions. Furthermore, lines also indicate that 王京 is a geopolitical target of “倭”. Evidence can be found in the lines including “倭逼王京” and “倭已入王京…劫王子”, these indicate that 王京 is an important target of “倭” where important personnel such as王子 were located. Thus, to conclude, 王京 has a high probability of functioning as an important geopolitical or military target of “倭”.
-   Another key word chosen to be discussed is “新”. While the other collocates are usually two words, “新” is an often co‑occurring single word with the target word. When evaluating “新” in the sentence, it has no clear function when standing alone. Looking into the work of Tognini-Bonelli may give insight on how to evaluate this single word throughout the sentence. Tognini-Bonelli (2001) argued that specific vocabulary or words may not merely serve as general grammar but can differ in meaning depending on their contextual use. Applying this argument to the case and closely investigating the lines associated with “新” and “倭”, the two words should be interpreted together. Evidence in the lines including “新倭复犯石坪” and “新倭，突犯嘉兴” indicates that the term usually associates with military action like “复犯” and “突犯”, suggesting that “新倭” represents a military force. With usual meaning of the Chinese word “新” as new, “新倭” has a high probability of representing a newly arrived force or military reinforcement of “倭”. 
-   In conclusion, this study investigated how the term “倭” was perceived in the Ming Shi. The study examined several aspects of collocation analysis, including the comparison between significance and strength of collocates; the comparison of collocates across different windows and key words in context. However, only one segmentation – jieba – was used in the study, which marks a limitation in the study, and it is suggested that multiple segmentations should be tested before deciding on an appropriate segmentation. In addition, the KWIC lines in the study suggest that “倭” sometimes aligns with other single words as a broader term. More examples can be found in a user dictionary generated by jieba, such as “倭人” and “倭使”. In this case, analyzing “倭” alone may not represent the whole picture of this group as viewed in Ming Shi. Therefore, future research should expand the scope of analysis to understand how this group was conceptualized in historical texts.
